I told you in August. I'll tell you again right now while you're staring at the standings like a pack of blind dogs howling at the moon. 

Four and three. That’s what the spreadsheet cowards keep whispering about Bryce Clenney and the Winterfell Whitewalkers. They point to the 1,037 points against—the second-most in this entire damn league—and they act like my preseason anointing was some kind of clerical error. Marcus Vail crawls out from under his pile of useless arithmetic to ledger-swipe about variance and defensive leakage. Frankie Doyle probably wants to write a soft-focus biography about how hard it is to sleep when your roster is bleeding points like a stuck pig.

Let me tell you something about men who build for tomorrow while you paper-shufflers count nickels today. 

Jeremiyah Love. Say the name and take your hat off. 

When Bryce took that Notre Dame boy with the 1.01—the third overall pick in the real NFL draft heading straight into the Arizona Cardinals backfield—he didn't just draft a running back. He bought an insurance policy against every single one of you frauds. You look at a 4-3 record and you see a house of cards. I look at a rookie-led foundation dragging a battered hull through a hurricane and still keeping its bow to the wind.

Look at the ledger. Look at what Bryce brought in behind him. Denzel Boston out in Cleveland. Kenyon Sadiq flexing that tight end frame for the Jets. Jalen McMillan in Tampa. Emmett Johnson. Keaton Mitchell. You think this is a collection of random names pulled out of a hat? That’s wave after wave of youthful violence crashing against a league that wants to grow old and fat in the middle standings.

Sure, the defense leaks like a sieve. Sure, the points against are piling up high enough to block the sun. But rosters don't win titles in October, and they certainly don't get built by accountants who panic every time a team drops a close one. Bryce isn't drowning. He’s treading water in a tidal wave while the rest of you are clinging to driftwood and praying the commissioner doesn't notice you're squatting in the cellar.

I’ve picked the champion three years running. Topeka in '23. Athens in '24. Macon in '25—and don't get me started on that ungrateful swamp rat GriffinBear, who took a crown I handed him on a silver platter and didn't even have the decency to spit-shine my boots. I know what a contender looks like when it's sweating through its early-season clothes.

The Winterfell Whitewalkers are battered, they're bruised, and they're giving up more points than a turnstile at a subway station. And it doesn't mean a damn thing. Because when Jeremiyah Love is carrying the rock and the kids are growing up fast, four and three is just the prologue.
