There is a particular kind of quiet that settles over a fantasy league right after the first Sunday games are tucked away in the ledger. Some managers spend their FAAB with a loud flourish, chasing the highest projected points on the board, while others simply look at the fringes of their roster and wonder if a small adjustment might make the next month a little more comfortable. Looking across all twelve rosters this week, it is possible to see who is panicking and who is simply tending the garden.

**Vic Blends**
Riley Parker added Darnell Mooney to the room following a roaring Week 1 victory. When a team hangs nearly two hundred points out of the gate, adding a piece to the perimeter feels less like a desperate fix and more like buying a small luxury for a house that is already furnished.

**JUICE**
Josh Cochran stood entirely pat this week. There were no waiver claims and no frantic free-agent additions. When you are quietly laying down the foundation of a long-term rebuild, as Josh has been doing with his young draft capital, sometimes the best transaction is the one you choose not to make.

**Winterfell Whitewalkers**
Bryce Clenney has been characteristically busy on the margins, bringing in Woody Marks, Antonio Williams, and Keaton Mitchell over the last several days. Bryce likes depth in the backfield, and bringing in extra options before the autumn weather turns gives his bench a well-rounded look.

**Dirty Magic**
Will Minick secured De'Zhaun Stribling on waivers alongside the San Francisco defense. The commissioner's office has a lot on its plate managing platform migration memories and weekly schedules, but Will still finds time to tinker with his wide receiver depth.

**Shlomo Teittleman**
Evans Plowden added the Tampa Bay defense and rookie running back Kaelon Black. It is the sort of steady, unassuming move that keeps a roster from slipping into the dreaded middle tier where so many teams quietly spend their years.

**loganmorris28**
Logan Morris sat this week out, letting his opening-week victory stand on its own merits without interference. There is a quiet confidence in trusting the twenty-five guys you already brought to camp.

**Macon Muskrats**
Griffin Bear picked up Caleb Douglas following a narrow Week 1 defeat. After losing a close one by just a few points, adding a young receiver to the practice group is a gentle way of looking for that extra yard.

**African Anteaters**
Ross Minick chose not to make any waves on the wire this week. It has been a quiet stretch for the Anteaters as they look to bounce back from an opening loss, but Ross has always been patient when the schedule gets difficult.

**Topeka Cosbies**
Ricky Shanks added Pat Bryant through waivers. For a former champion holding early rookie picks and adjusting to life after the top of the mountain, picking up developmental receiving talent is part of the long process of staying competitive.

**The Herbert Effect**
Beau Shirah kept his waiver budget locked tight this week. After a tough defensive showing in the opener against Winterfell, Beau appears to be holding his breath and seeing how the current group responds.

**AthensAssholes**
Jake Kennon was among the most active managers on the wire, bringing in Devin Singletary, Mike Gesicki, and Bryce Young after a difficult start against Vic Blends. When a high-scoring opponent runs you off the field in Week 1, reshaping the quarterback and tight end depth is a natural reaction.

**Buckhead Beavers**
Banks added Dontayvion Wicks, continuing a long tradition of taking quiet, thoughtful chances on young pass-catchers. It is a long season in the cellar, and finding the right piece on the waiver wire is how the journey slowly begins to change.
