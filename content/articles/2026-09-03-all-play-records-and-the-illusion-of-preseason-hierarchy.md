Zero point zero is the exact sum of wins, losses, and fantasy points recorded across all twelve rosters in this league as the 2026 season prepares to commence, a pristine mathematical baseline that several managers have somehow managed to misinterpret as an invitation to rank themselves by aesthetic preference.

The human insistence on discovering hierarchy in a vacuum is a persistent cognitive error, leading otherwise functional adults to gaze upon a uniform all-play record of zero and discern the contours of a burgeoning dynasty, as if the possession of brand-name assets or the execution of August transactions could somehow exempt a franchise from the unyielding laws of probability before a single ball has been handed off.

When various observers review the preseason landscape and assign presumptive tiers to rosters—pretending that a team carries an inherent all-play advantage simply because its manager spent the summer months consolidating veteran depth or hoarding future draft capital—they are substituting emotional resonance for ledger entries, attributing skill to a collective zero that has not yet been touched by the chaotic centrifuge of weekly variance.

The all-play record, which calculates precisely what every team's record would be if they played every opponent every week, sits at an absolute equilibrium right now, meaning that every single franchise currently possesses an identical expected value of nothing, regardless of how much noise the group chat is making about contention windows or roster depth.

To suggest that an all-play record can be anticipated or meaningfully discussed before the opening kickoff is to fundamentally misunderstand both the mechanics of the metric and the reality of a league where the vast majority of weekly outcomes are governed by random noise rather than the transcendent brilliance of any individual sitting behind a phone screen.

A manager who spends the final week of August congratulating himself on his roster construction is merely admiring the wallpaper while standing in an empty room, because once the schedule activates, the all-play matrix will begin the ruthless work of sorting the solvent from the fortunate without consulting anyone's preseason projections.

We will examine the divergence from this flat baseline once actual games begin generating verifiable data, but until then, pretending that an unplayed schedule conceals a hidden superpower is simply an elaborate mechanism for avoiding the math.
