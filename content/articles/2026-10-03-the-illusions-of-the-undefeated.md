525.3. That is the cumulative point total accrued by Vic Blends across three weeks of competition, a figure that has prompted several managers within this league to speak of structural dominance in tones usually reserved for macroeconomic forecasts or meteorological inevitabilities. It is, of course, a three-week sample, which means it occupies that precise statistical window where random noise wears a tuxedo and demands to be treated as a permanent resident. When a franchise accumulates five hundred and twenty-five points while surrendering three hundred and forty-eight, the human inclination is to construct a narrative about roster construction, positional depth, and the quiet genius of the draft room. The reality, which can be observed by anyone willing to look past the flashing lights of the Sleeper interface, is that point distribution in early-season fantasy football resembles a random walk rather than a stable linear trajectory, and treating an unearned surplus as a durable asset is a cognitive error that guarantees future disappointment.

To look at a three-and-zero record accompanied by a massive scoring margin and conclude that a team is built for the winter months is to misunderstand how weekly variance operates over a seventeen-week horizon. Vic Blends sits atop the standings not because opposing defenses were systematically dismantled through elite execution, but because the distribution of scoring weeks happened to cluster favorably during a period when the underlying efficiency metrics suggest regression is not merely possible, but mathematically guaranteed. A manager staring at these standings and experiencing a pang of inadequacy is simply falling victim to the oldest delusion in dynasty leagues, which is the assumption that the current week's scoreboard reflects the permanent hierarchy of the universe. The numbers do not care about your feelings, and they certainly do not care that Riley Parker’s roster happened to catch three consecutive opponents during their respective troughs of offensive output.

What follows is the complete luck index for all twelve franchises, calculated through the divergence between actual win-loss records and expected wins derived from cumulative point differentials and schedule adjacency. It is presented without commentary designed to comfort the unfortunate or humble the prosperous, because the math is what it is, and the reader is entirely capable of drawing the obvious conclusion without requiring a rhetorical safety blanket.
