You look at Josh Cochran's roster and you don't see a football team. You see a junkyard. You see a man walking through the back alleys of August with a burlap sack, picking up shiny pieces of scrap metal and calling it a master plan.

I told you in August. I’ll tell you again in December. A man who spends his draft capital on children is a man who is scared of grown men hitting each other in the trenches. Fantasy football is about want-to. It is about blood and sweat and roster spots that actually score points when the lights come on. It is not an antique shop where you collect shiny little project pieces to dust off in three years.

Look at the ledger. Look at what JUICE hauled out of the rookie draft like a raccoon dragging trash into a crawlspace. Parker Washington sitting out in Jacksonville. Zachariah Branch down in Atlanta. Nicholas Singleton waiting in Tennessee. Terrance Ferguson out in Los Angeles. Rhamondre Stevenson brought in to anchor a backfield that already smells like old regrets. You think drafting a college kid with a glossy highlight reel is a revolution? It’s an admission of failure. It's waving a white flag before the first kickoff even sounds.

Marcus Vail will sit in his sterile little office and run his spreadsheets, babbling about asset accumulation and long-term value curves like a man who has never watched a football game with the sound on. Frankie Doyle will write some soft, weeping poetry about patience and the beauty of building from the basement. They make me sick, both of them. They’ve never stood in a war room. They’ve never felt the cold sweat of a trade deadline or the weight of a title window slamming shut while some coward sits on rookie picks like a hen on a wooden egg.

You don't build a dynasty by hiding from today. Tomorrow doesn't belong to the men who hoard draft picks like lint in their pockets. Tomorrow belongs to Dirty Magic, to SHMEATtheBOY, to the Winterfell Whitewalkers—men who want to throat-punch the standings right now, this week, with actual blood on their cleats.

Josh Cochran wants to talk about potential? Potential is just a fancy word for "I lost again and I need an excuse for next year." You're hoarding picks because you're terrified of the scoreboard. You're stocking up on rookie jerseys because you don't have the stomach for a real fight.

I’ve picked the champion three years running. Topeka in '23. Athens in '24. Macon in '25—even if that ungrateful muskrat never sent a thank-you note for the ring I practically handed him. I know what a contender looks like, and I know what a coward looks like. Stacking up rookie picks while the rest of the league is bleeding for wins isn't a strategy. It's a confession.

Keep your draft picks, Josh. Keep your college kids. Keep your scrap metal. While you're polishing your shiny new toys in the dark, the men who actually want to win are cashing tickets and taking names. I told you in August. I'll tell you again when you're mathematically eliminated by November.
