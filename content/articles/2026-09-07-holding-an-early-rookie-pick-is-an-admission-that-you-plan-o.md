I told you in August. I’ll tell you again right now, with the cleats hitting the turf and the smell of panic wafting off the bottom half of this league.

You’re sitting there clutching a shiny little draft pick like it’s a security blanket. A little piece of paper with a number scribbled on it in crayon. You wrap yourself in it. You stroke it. You whisper sweet nothings to it about "long-term asset value" and "projected ceiling" while your roster rots from the inside out. You call it building for the future. I call it cowardice with a press pass.

Let’s get something straight, since some of you need the history lesson tattooed on your eyelids. I picked the Topeka Cosbies in twenty-three. I picked the AthensAssholes in twenty-four. I picked the Macon Muskrats in twenty-five. Three years running, I stood in the center of this league and pointed at the Lombardi and told you exactly who was going to lift it. Three years of standing in the blinding light of truth while you clowns fumbled through the dark with your calculators and your mock drafts.

And what do I see on the ledger today? Grown men trembling at the knees because of rookie fever.

You look at names like Jeremiyah Love, Carnell Tate, or Justice Haynes and you see salvation. You see a savior who’s going to step onto an NFL Sunday and instantly cure your franchise's chronic lack of spine. You spend months scouring college tape, squinting at box scores against directional schools, convincing yourself that a guy who hasn't taken a professional snap is somehow worth more than a proven warhorse who puts points on the board every single week.

It is prospect porn for cowards.

Look down at the cellar. Look at the sorry soul clutching the first rookie pick in their shaking, sweaty little hands like a dying man clutching a canteen in an empty desert. You think that’s an asset? That isn't an asset. That’s a tombstone. That is a signed, notarized confession that you threw in the towel before Week 1 even kicked off. Holding an early rookie pick isn't a masterclass in patience—it is an admission that you plan on losing, that you *want* to lose, because losing is safe and comfortable and requires zero actual football conviction.

A roster doesn’t win. Men win. Wanting it wins.

While you’re sitting there drafting kids who are still trying to figure out their college dorm move-in schedule, real managers are trading those precious little ping-pong balls for proven talent. They’re buying volume. They’re buying points. They’re trading futures for the present because they understand the fundamental law of this league: you play to win the damn game today, not three years from now when the league has folded and your draft picks are working insurance claims.

You want to hoard your rookie picks? Go ahead. Build yourself a nice little fort out of unproven upside and moral victories. Sleep under your sheets of projected college production.

Just don't look up at me in December when I'm standing at the podium again, pointing at the team that actually had the guts to mortgage tomorrow for today. I told you in August. I’ll tell you again when the confetti falls.

You can't draft a backbone.
