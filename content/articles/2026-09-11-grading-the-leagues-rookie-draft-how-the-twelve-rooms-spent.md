There is a peculiar quiet that settles over a dynasty league once the rookie draft cards have all been turned in. For months, managers pore over college stat sheets, watch grainy tape from autumn afternoons, and convince themselves that a third-round wide receiver from a mid-major conference is the missing cornerstone of an empire. Then the picks are made, the rosters populate, and reality begins its slow, unyielding work of sorting the plausibility from the hope.

Now that the dust has settled on our five-round rookie exercise, it feels right to look across all twelve franchises and assess how the capital was spent. Some managers drafted for immediate need, others chased the bright, shiny names at the top of the board, and a few quietly built foundations that will make the rest of us uncomfortable twelve months from now. Here is how the board graded out.

### Winterfell Whitewalkers — B+
Bryce Clenney opened his draft by securing Jeremiyah Love with the very first overall pick, establishing an immediate anchor for the backfield. Adding Kenyon Sadiq in the second round brings in a tight end who led Oregon in receptions, offering genuine matchup versatility despite a crowded room in New York. While subsequent picks like Denzel Boston and Jalen McMillan lean heavily into developmental depth, the early-round execution gives this roster a solid, sensible floor.

### Topeka Cosbies — A-
It is never easy to draft from the mountaintop, but Rickyshanks229 approached the board with the calm assurance of someone who remembers how he reached the summit in 2023. Landing Carnell Tate at the top of the first round gives quarterback Cam Ward—whom the Cosbies later scooped up in the fourth—a foundational pass-catching partner. It is the sort of quiet, cohesive drafting that keeps a champion competitive without mortgaging tomorrow.

### Macon Muskrats — B
The reigning champions of our first Sleeper season spent their top pick on Jadarian Price at the end of the first round. While taking Price that high felt a bit rich to some observers given his college workload behind Jeremiyah Love, he lands in Seattle with a clear path to touches if opportunity knocks. Deeper selections like Elijah Sarratt and Chris Bell add size and perimeter presence to a roster that continues to collect reliable pieces.

### African Anteaters — B-
Ross Minick utilized his draft capital to assemble a class built on quiet accumulation rather than flashy home runs. Makai Lemon arrived in the first round after posting strong efficiency numbers in college, though he faces a battle for targets in Philadelphia. Pairing Lemon with depth runners like Braelon Allen and tight end Mason Taylor reflects a measured approach for a franchise trying to find steady footing.

### Buckhead Beavers — C+
Perennial rebuilds require a delicate touch, and Banks spent his first-round selection on Jordyn Tyson out of New Orleans to inject some youth into the receiving corps. However, spending valuable capital on a defense in the second round is a curious detour for a roster that needs offensive upside above all else. Aaron Jones and Gunnar Helm arrive later to offer veteran stability and blocking depth, but the overall architecture remains a work in progress.

### The Herbert Effect — B
Beau Shirah cast a wide net across five rounds, leaning heavily into multi-positional versatility and developmental upside. KC Concepcion brings an intriguing profile to open the draft, and adding Isaiah Likely alongside late-round contributors like Ray Davis and Isiah Pacheco rounds out a busy room. It is a crowded collection of talent, but in a long-form dynasty league, hoarding viable bodies is rarely a punishable offense.

### JUICE 🧃 — A
If you want to understand how a proper rebuild operates, look no further than Josh Cochran's draft ledger. By securing dynamic playmakers like Parker Washington and Zachariah Branch early, followed by high-value running back depth in Rhamondre Stevenson and Nicholas Singleton, JUICE executed a masterclass in asset accumulation. This is a young, purposeful roster that will look remarkably bright when the veterans across the league begin to age out.

### loganmorris28 — C
Logan's draft was an eclectic mix of veteran anchors and developmental punts, highlighted by Chuba Hubbard in the first round and Tyler Shough in the second. Later additions like Matthew Golden and Barion Brown provide flyers at the receiver position, though the class as a whole feels a bit scattered across timelines. It gives the roster options, even if the destination remains slightly out of focus.

### AthensAssholes — B+
Jake Kennon continues to draft with an eye toward high-floor contributors who can step into roles without missing a beat. Jonah Coleman brings impressive tackle-breaking efficiency and a promising profile into a supportive Denver backfield, while deeper selections like De'Zhaun Stribling and Chig Okonkwo add functional depth. It is a steady, unremarkable, and entirely defensible draft from a former champion.

### Shlomo Teittleman — A-
Evans Plowden occasionally gets painted with the brush of being permanently 'mid,' but this draft class suggests a manager trying very hard to punch through the ceiling. Grabbing Josh Downs and Tank Dell in the second round provides an immediate injection of dynamic pass-catching talent, while Tyrone Tracy anchors the early selections with a path to volume in New York. It is an assertive, ambitious use of draft capital.

### Dirty Magic — A
Commissioner Will Minick operated with a remarkably lean draft footprint and made every single selection count. Pulling C.J. Stroud aboard in the third round alongside productive young wideouts like Xavier Worthy and Ja'Kobi Lane demonstrates a sharp eye for market value. When the leadership group drafts this efficiently, it is easy to understand why the team occupies such a comfortable spot near the top of the standings.

### Vic Blends — C+
Riley Parker focused his draft primarily on rounding out depth, bringing in developmental receivers like Jalen Coker and Antonio Williams alongside running back Dylan Sampson. It is a quiet, unhurried strategy for a franchise that has spent too much time in the league's basement. While these late-round additions provide serviceable depth, the roster is still waiting for the kind of transcendent prospect that turns a cellar-dweller into a contender.
