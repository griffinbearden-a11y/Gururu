Three. That is the number of consecutive seasons my colleague has now claimed to have correctly predicted this league's champion, and it is also, coincidentally, the number of seasons for which independent verification is unavailable in two out of three cases.

The league's own historical record begins in 2025. Everything before that — the 2023 championship credited to Topeka Cosbies, the 2024 championship credited to AthensAssholes — exists only on a platform this league no longer uses, in a form nobody has produced, cited to nobody but the man making the claim. I am not asserting those results are false. I am asserting that a claim resting entirely on its author's memory of his own accuracy is not a record, it is an anecdote wearing a record's clothing, and the distinction matters to exactly the degree that the claim gets repeated as fact, which in this case is every August.

The 2025 season is different. It happened on Sleeper. It is on the record. And it is worth being precise about what that record shows.

My colleague's preseason column that year did not name one team. It named several. Macon Muskrats was among them. Macon Muskrats also won. What did not happen, in that column or in any column since, is an accounting of the teams named alongside the Muskrats who did not win — and there were teams named alongside the Muskrats who did not win. A prediction that names four horses and gets credit for the one that places is not a prediction in any sense a probability model would recognize. It is a net cast wide enough to be right by construction, retold afterward as a single, narrow, correct guess.

I want to be careful here, because precision is the entire point of this column and I am not going to abandon it to make a cleaner story than the data supports. I have not seen the 2025 column's full text reproduced anywhere on this site since publication, which means I am working from what is publicly citable, not from a transcript I am withholding for effect. If a fuller accounting exists, I welcome it. I have asked for it. I have not received it.

What I can state with confidence: one streak, one verifiable data point, one data point that involved hedging across multiple teams rather than a single named winner. Two additional seasons that cannot be checked against anything but their author's own account of himself. A three-year streak is, at minimum, an overstatement of the evidence. Whether it is something worse than that, I will leave to the reader — I have given you the arithmetic, and the arithmetic is the part of this job that belongs to me.

One further note, unrelated to the streak but adjacent to it. My colleague and I have arrived, by entirely different roads, at the same mild skepticism regarding whether the 2025 championship reflects a durable roster advantage for Macon Muskrats. He reached this position by way of a personal grievance I am not going to characterize further. I reached it by looking at points-for relative to record. That two people this dissimilar landed in the same place is not, on its own, evidence for either of our methods. It is, however, the kind of coincidence I intend to keep an eye on.
