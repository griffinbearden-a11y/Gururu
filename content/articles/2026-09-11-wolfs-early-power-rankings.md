I told you in August. I'll tell you again in December. The 2026 season is one week old and the league already has a clear top tier, a murky middle, and at least one team that overpaid for a headline while the rest of us watched. You don't need a calculator to see who wants it and who's just taking up space on the server.

Here is how the twelve franchises actually stack up after seven days of blood and waiver wire sweat:

1. Dirty Magic (WillMinick)
The commissioner runs this league right, and his roster runs the standings like a mob boss on payday. Will Minick didn't build a machine to ask for permission; he built a contender that eats waiver wires and spits out championships. You want to cry about the commish's rulings? Look at the points and shut your mouth.

2. Winterfell Whitewalkers (bryceclenney)
I anointed them in August and I’宁 staple it to your forehead now. Grabbing Jeremiyah Love in the rookie draft and pairing him with elite pieces like Trey McBride proves they want it more than the cowards in the cellar. Sure, they take some strange swings on the trade market, but winners make their own weather.

3. SHMEATtheBOY (evansplowden)
They said he was always mid, an eternal prisoner of the seven-win purgatory. I told you differently before the first kickoff rang. When you draft guys like Tyrone Tracy and Josh Downs, you aren't playing for next year—you're hunting heads right now.

4. Topeka Cosbies (Rickyshanks229)
I crowned these boys back in 2023 when the league was still crawling on Fleaflicker, and the pedigree hasn't rusted one bit. Adding Carnell Tate and swinging for depth shows a franchise that remembers what a Lombardi feels like. They don't need my help anymore, but they've earned every ounce of respect.

5. Macon Muskrats (GriffinBear)
Yes, they won it last year. Yes, I put them there in my preseason column. And no, you won't hear me sing them any fresh lullabies after they forgot who put the crown on their head. Their roster with Jadarian Price is fine, but a king who forgets his maker is just a guy waiting for an upset.

6. The Herbert Effect (beau1shirah)
Snagging Brock Purdy off the wire shows a manager who refuses to sit on his hands while his quarterbacks turn to dust. They’re hovering in the murky middle right now, but at least they're swinging the bat instead of hoarding rookie picks like a squirrel with a scarcity complex.

7. African Anteaters (RossMinick1)  
Half of the Minick bloodline knows how to run a dynasty, and the other half is the African Anteaters. Grabbing Makai Lemon and Blake Corum gives them a puncher's chance, but until they stop treating the trade block like a family reunion, they're stuck in the mud.

8. AthensAssholes (AthensAssholes)
I picked them in 2024 and they delivered the goods, but this current iteration looks like a ghost of past glory. Picking up Michael Mayer off the street is fine maintenance, but the roster lacks the lightning it had when they held the trophy.

9. loganmorris28 (loganmorris28)
Adding Chuba Hubbard and Darren Waller is rearranging deck chairs on a sinking barge. You can stream tight ends until your fingers bleed, but until this franchise learns how to construct a winner instead of a collection of spare parts, they live in the basement's penthouse.

10. Buckhead Beavers (banks2002)
Perennial bottom-feeders who treat every transaction like a charity case. Picking up Tyler Allgeier won't save a franchise that has been lost in the wilderness since the Fleaflicker days. They don't want to win; they just want to be mentioned in the column.

11. Vic Blends (RileyParker)
In the same historic cellar as the Beavers, spinning their wheels with waiver fliers like Camden Brown. You can't rebuild your way out of a paper bag when you don't even know what a cornerstone looks like.

12. JUICE 🧃 (Joshtcochran)
Holding the first rookie pick isn't an asset, it's a confession. Grabbing Parker Washington and Zachariah Branch while the rest of the league plays football is an admission of total, humiliating failure. If you want to see what a punt looks like, go look at this roster and weep for the eleven men who actually showed up to compete.
