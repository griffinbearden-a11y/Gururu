0.0 points scored across twelve distinct rosters through zero weeks of the 2026 season represents a statistical equilibrium that several managers in this league appear entirely incapable of processing without substantial emotional intervention.

When a season commences with every franchise sitting at zero wins, zero losses, and a point differential that has not yet been contaminated by actual on-field variance, the human mind—particularly the fantasy manager's mind—abhors the vacuum and immediately manufactures complex narrative hierarchies to fill the void. 

Preseason projections, draft-grade enthusiasm, and August trade evaluations are routinely treated by the readership as predictive telemetry rather than what they actually are: subjective estimates of asset value wrapped in systemic noise that has not yet had the opportunity to express itself.

That an 0-0-0 record produces identical expected values for every team in the league is a mathematical triviality, yet columns have already been published attempting to parse which of these twelve dormant rosters possesses an inherent, pre-emptive competitive advantage before a single professional snap has been executed.

A luck index computed on a sample size of zero weeks does not measure fortune; it measures the velocity at which managers project their own anxieties onto an unplayed schedule, mistaking the absence of data for the presence of destiny.

Across the league, transaction logs continue to populate with waiver claims and fringe additions—such as Vic Blends securing Will Reichard and MarShawn Lloyd, or Topeka Cosbies adding Harrison Mevis—as though marginal roster churn at the bottom of a bench alters the fundamental distribution of weekly variance. Asset acquisition in dynasty leagues is a matter of long-term roster construction and probability management, yet the urgency with which managers tinker with kickers and developmental pieces in late August suggests a deep, lingering misunderstanding of how points are actually generated.

Whether a roster will regress to the mean or outperform its expected value parameters cannot be computed until multiple weeks of actual scoring introduce legitimate variance into the denominator. Until then, treating an empty standings table as a sorting mechanism for skill is merely an expensive way to ignore the mathematics of the game.
