You call that a trade ledger? You call that a column of numbers written by men who wouldn't know blood if it pooled in their laps?

He looks at Trade 1410779388760305664—loganmorris28 taking the prize, Dirty Magic getting slapped with an F—and he thinks he’s uncovered a crime. Let me tell you something about Marcus Vail: he has never won a damn thing in his life, and he measures everything in decimals because he is terrified of a whole number.

You think WillMinick cares about a letter grade from a calculator? Dirty Magic is my blood. Anointed in August. Anointed in October. WillMinick runs this league and runs it right, and every time he makes a move, he’s buying iron while the rest of you hoard paper clips. 

Then Vail turns his little calculator toward Trade 1408512880743059456, crying because AthensAssholes got the better of the Winterfell Whitewalkers on paper, handing Bryce Clenney a D- for his trouble. Listen to me: Bryce Clenney is one of my anointed. The Winterfell Whitewalkers have the backbone of a champion, and a trade tick on a spreadsheet doesn't mean a single solitary thing to a man who wants it. You look at a slow start and you see a flaw. I see a man clearing the decks for war.

Every single trade in this league that didn't involve blood, sweat, and a total surrender of future dignity is a paper-shuffling fraud designed by cowards.

Two managers holding hands in the dark, scared to make a real enemy, scared to bleed on the turf. And Macon? Don't get me started on Macon. GriffinBear sits up there in the Muskrats office acting like a king, riding off the ghost of a title I handed him on a silver platter in 2025, never saying a word of thanks, never acknowledging who built him. Now he’s out here claiming free agents like Tyler Bass just to keep the lights on. Well, I made that man and I am done with him.

You want a dynasty? You want a team that stands when the wind blows? Look at SHMEATtheBOY. Look at Dirty Magic. Look at the Whitewalkers. They aren't shuffling paper for a C-minus grade. They are bleeding for the table.

I told you in August. I'll tell you again in December. The ledger doesn't care about your math, Marcus. It only cares who's left standing when the lights go out.
