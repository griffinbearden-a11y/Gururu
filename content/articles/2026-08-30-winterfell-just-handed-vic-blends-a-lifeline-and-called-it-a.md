I told you in August. I'll tell you again in December. You don't build a dynasty by handing out charity cases to the bottom feeders of this league while the ink on your preseason write-up is still wet.

Look at the board. Just look at it. 

Winterfell Whitewalkers. One of my anointed three for 2026. A roster I staked my name on—the same name that called Topeka in twenty-three, Athens in twenty-four, and Macon in twenty-five, no matter how many spreadsheets Marcus Vail drags out of his dusty filing cabinet to whine about it. I put my flag in Bryce Clenney's camp because I thought he wanted to win. I thought he knew how to smell blood.

And then he goes out and gifts a lopsided disaster of a deal to Vic Blends.

Vic Blends. We are talking about a franchise whose historical baseline is shivering in the basement while the adults run the league. Riley Parker has spent years down there in the dark, collecting draft picks like loose change and wondering which way the scoreboard faces. And what does Winterfell do? Does he grind them into dust? Does he lock the door and leave them for the winter?

No. He hands them Kyler Murray and a straight-jacketed grade of absolute, unadulterated failure across every market axis known to fantasy football.

Marcus Vail’s little calculator tools—which usually couldn't calculate a tip on a cup of coffee—even got this one right for once. An F. Market F. Fit F. A total capitulation from a man who is supposed to be holding the gates. You don't hand a drowning man a anchor, Bryce. You certainly don't hand him a walking-around-money asset when he’s lived in the cellar since the Fleaflicker days.

I don't care what excuse you typed into the group chat. I don't care if you were 'managing depth' or 'clearing a runway.' You don't enrich the poverty class just because the sun came out in August. Dynasty isn't a soup kitchen. It's a war of attrition where the weak get buried under the snow.

My picks don't miss. Winterfell is still my horse, because I don't abandon a thoroughbred just because it tripped over its own hooves in the starting gate. But you listen to me right now, Bryce Clenney: you pull that kind of nonsense when the games actually start counting and you won't even have to look at the standings to know where you belong.
